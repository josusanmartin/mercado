**Mercado v6 — raised-bar taste critique**

Review: 14 October 2026, starting 08:55 UTC. Reviewer: `review`. Application read-only; no signatures, paid calls, or transactions. Ranked for a judge’s first 30 seconds, then the rest of a three-minute demonstration.

Mercado now has a recognizable visual identity. The sarape lettering, terracotta Remesas, cream Card and maize Invest are worth keeping. It still does not consistently feel like a finished, top-tier financial product. The opening demonstrates branding before it demonstrates a financial system; navigation changes scenes without much continuity; some new animation treats an idle clock like an important event. The remaining gap is composition and meaningful behavior, not a shortage of effects.

V6 is already improving this. During capture, Global acquired a compact flow panel, Invest acquired an allocation donut, and Home acquired the Tempo proof tiles. These are substantial improvements over the sentence tables. The next bar is that every visual answers a question, every movement has a cause, and every financial mark has an honest unit and scope.

**1. Home: show one connected economy before asking the judge to explore six products.**

The opening is an impressive poster followed by a directory. At desktop size, “Built on Tempo” starts around y=1052 and “How the money moves” around y=1554 in the light capture. The material that explains the thesis is outside the opening viewport. A judge can remember the stripes and six names without understanding why these are one system.

**Exact change — web:** add a compact scene, at most 170px tall, between the hero/Tour action and the index. Put xMXN at its center; connect issuance, exchange/delivery, credit, spending and saving with labeled paths. Include Global as an exchange branch. Show one real recent receipt with amount, source time and an explorer link; each other number remains explicitly its own product’s metric. Use the existing activity hooks. Do not imply that unrelated demo accounts or transactions form one trace. With no receipt, show a static mechanism and “No recent transfer to show.” Keep the six-product index below. The first screen should answer: what is the shared asset, where does it go, and what can I inspect?

Evidence: [desktop Home](v6-evidence/stills/1440-light/1440-light-normal-home.png); `components/home/ecosystem-home.tsx:110–120` places the index, proof section and map in that order. Lead approved this upgrade at 08:56; implementation is in flight.

**2. Motion: idle, quoted, pending and confirmed must look different.**

The new kit is useful, but its first integration gives some motion the wrong meaning. Remesas sets `active={!!r || quoted}`: a quote moves money visually, and a completed receipt keeps moving forever. Global initially pulsed the expiry countdown once per second. Invest initially glowed its projected earnings every second. Multiple unrelated loops make the product look busy without making it feel responsive.

**Exact change — web/api/mobile2:** expose an explicit visual state on FlowPath: `idle | quoted | pending | confirmed | failed`. Quoted is a still path; pending gets a constant-speed travelling marker; confirmed plays one approximately 450ms arrival and stops; failed stops at the relevant stage. Use `linear` for continuous travel, `cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)` for arrivals, and 120–180ms feedback for direct actions. Pulse only after a meaningful value or event changes, deduplicated by quote/receipt identity. Do not pulse expiry, “seconds ago,” or the 50ms projected-interest counter. Keep the live decimals. Suspend offscreen loops; reduced motion renders the final state immediately.

The same rule applies to Home’s new MoneyArc: historical dots should be a finite, labeled replay, not perpetual evidence of new transfers. The old map’s decorative circulation must be labeled as a mechanism. Do not label average block spacing as the measured confirmation time of a particular payment.

Evidence: archived `remesas/remesas-screen.tsx:116–120`, `lib/motion/index.tsx:179–216`, `app/globals.css:473–487`, `global/global-screen.tsx:573`; [motion sequence](v6-evidence/motion/1440-dark/1440-dark.webm). Lead approved at 08:58. API reported removing the countdown Pulse; mobile2 reported replacing recurring glow with a one-shot deposit/withdrawal effect. API later reported these changes committed on kit `69fe146` (its final API-page commit `aabbcc3`); mobile2 reported `3cde1b5`. These acknowledgements are not a new end-to-end gate.

**3. Phone Home: spend the viewport on understanding, not a four-line slogan.**

At 390×844 the headline occupies about 194px, and only Mint is fully visible above the footer/navigation. The money-map heading is around y=2084 in dark mode. The menu is usable; the first screen is still poorly allocated.

**Exact change — web/mobile:** below 640px, set the Home headline to `clamp(2rem,10cqw,2.5rem)` so “One currency.” and “Six products.” each fit one line at 390px. Use 16px section gaps. Keep the Tour action 48px tall. Give product rows a 72px minimum with name and metric sharing a baseline; put the purpose on the second line and allow growth for real content. Fit the compact scene from #1 plus two complete product entries above the honesty area. Do not reduce disclosures or touch targets to win the fold. This is a Home composition change, not a global shrinking of the native phone apps.

Evidence: [phone Home](v6-evidence/stills/390-light/390-light-normal-home.png), `ecosystem-home.tsx:79–103,155–175`. Lead approved at 08:59.

**4. Dark mode: a color that works for text should not automatically fill a whole hero.**

Global’s dark hero becomes a broad pale periwinkle rectangle. It is readable, but its brightness competes with the action well. Mint’s original dark allocation strip has five increasingly dark green segments; several nearly disappear into the canvas. The cream Card treatment is intentional and works; leave its character intact.

**Exact change — web/contracts:** split `--accent-text`, `--accent-surface`, `--accent-chart-*` and their foreground tokens. For dark Global, retain `#8EA8FF` for small numbers/focus, use the existing rich cobalt `#2448C8` for the hero and white hero text. Derive the wash from the surface token, not the bright text token. For Mint, use five explicit light-to-dark jade chart steps in dark mode, 1px canvas separators and identical legend swatches. Keep proportional widths; a 0.3% holding is a sliver with a readable legend, not an inflated chunk. Check contrast on actual token pairs after the change.

**5. Charts: never make a plausible-looking line carry an unspecified financial claim.**

The new Invest graph is a step series of deposits/withdrawals/trades, starts from an assumed zero before the first observed event, and appends the current value. Its accessible name says “tu saldo en el tiempo.” Stocks displays a USD headline above a token-quantity plot. The screenshot has no visible quantity, axis labels or period. That is attractive chart furniture, not yet understandable financial information.

**Exact change — mobile2/api:** if using transaction events, call the series “Aportes netos · OUSD” / “Aportes netos · xMXN” or “mIDX comprados − vendidos.” State the actual observed date range; use “últimos 30 días” only if that is the query’s real coverage. Start at the first observed event. Do not attach current valuation to a contribution series; show current value separately. For a true balance/value chart, use timestamped balances/valuations in one unit and break unknown intervals. Add first/last dates and min/max values outside the plot, plus a focus/touch point readout. Zero, one point and incomplete history need explicit captions. Make the SVG accessible name match the visible quantity and range. Apply the same discipline to Crédito’s loan-book sparkline and its demo-earnings chart.

Evidence: [Stocks detail](v6-evidence/details/390-light-invest-stocks.png); archived `invest/phone-app.tsx:264–282,293–296,354`; `mobile/extra/editorial.tsx:674–718`. Lead approved at 08:59. This is a semantic upgrade to real data, not permission to invent a smoother history.

Home → Global currently replaces the composition, fades/slides the new route, then reveals the product letters. It is fast enough, but it reads as a collection of pages. Phone sub-screens have stronger directional continuity than the desktop shell.

**Exact change — web/mobile/mobile2:** give the persistent sidebar one active marker that moves to the new row in 180ms with the shared ease, rather than mounting unrelated selected backgrounds. Carry the selected product’s color into a persistent hero surface over 180–220ms. On client-side navigation, render the destination title as a complete word; reserve the letter entrance for a fresh landing. Crossfade the content group once, at most 160ms with 0–4px displacement. Keep fixed navigation outside transforms and retain the v5 transform-release fix. For phone back/forward, keep the existing directional screen transition and move only the content plane; metadata and navigation stay anchored. No new animation may delay an action becoming usable.

Evidence: [Home row](v6-evidence/motion/1440-light/1440-light-route-0ms.png), [destination entrance](v6-evidence/motion/1440-light/1440-light-route-180ms.png); `console/shell.tsx:57,80–102`, `console/settings.tsx:99–101,133`, `app/globals.css:344–370`. **7. Typography: make display type, money and evidence do different jobs.**

The desktop Global and Crédito bands still devote several lines to mechanism/disclaimer prose. On phone Global the band extends to roughly y=326 before tabs. Elsewhere tiny all-caps metadata carries long financial qualifiers. The result alternates between shouting and fine print.

**Exact change — web/api:** keep Archivo for the product word; use Inter Tight for action amounts and supporting numbers. Use a deliberate 48/32/24/16/13px hierarchy rather than scaling every label independently. Set financial explanations in sentence case, 13–14px with 1.4 line height; reserve tracked capitals for short section labels. Replace Global’s hero paragraph with **“Pay suppliers in xMXN from OUSD, or redeem xMXN into OUSD.”** Put **“Admin-relayed testnet FX feed”** next to the quote rate, and keep mint/burn mechanics in “How it works.” Align numbers and units on one baseline; retain eight decimal places in the explicitly live earnings treatment, with the fractional tail visually subordinate. Avoid compressing a safety qualifier into a narrow uppercase caption.

**8. Side panels: use the right visual grammar for each financial relationship.**

Replacing every paragraph with the same three-dot FlowPath would produce a more animated template, not a more intelligible system. A quote is a conversion; credit is a state machine plus capacity; a contract architecture is a permissions graph. They should not all look like money moving through a linear pipeline.

**Exact change — api/contracts:** Crédito should pair a discrete eligibility stepper with an explicitly illustrative score/floor gauge, then a separate pool-capacity bar showing cash, lent amount and the utilization cap. Keep “Demo Uber Data” and “illustrative score” attached to those visuals. Invest should lead with allocation in one common valuation unit and show realized/projected earnings separately. For Contracts, draw Factory → locked issuer roles, branch into Vault and Exchange → xMXN, then consuming products. Put admin-controlled feeds/risk parameters on dashed side branches; label arrows “grants role,” “mints,” or “uses.” A single linear Factory → currency → issuers arrow is ambiguous about authority. Keep verified-source status separate from trust/control status. For Developers, show discrete observed stages—challenge received, payment confirmed, response received—rather than filling all nodes because a request began.

The v6 FlowPath is cleaner than the old table, but repeats input, rate and output that the form already shows. The most useful additional visual would explain the difference between the oracle conversion, quoted receipt and minimum receipt.

**Exact change — api:** under the flow, add a compact three-row value bridge: **“At oracle mid” → “Quoted receipt” → “Minimum received.”** Show fee and size-impact deductions between the first two, using the quote engine’s returned terms and consistent units/rounding. Do not visually enlarge a tiny fee to make a dramatic bar. Use aligned numbers, a thin bracket and a 28–36px final amount; retain cost-vs-mid as a small percentage. Put expiry in a quiet 120px track with text, based on the actual quote deadline, and a clear stale state when it expires. No recurring flash or false urgency. Keep the action well’s established width, height and position through loading and refresh.

**11. Empty, loading, failed and stale are four different designs.**

V6’s illustrated empty states are welcome. The Home proof tile still has `loading` versus a value/dash, without an explicit error state. The arc says “No … swaps” when the filtered list is empty; a failed or bounded read must not visually become proof of no activity. A returning judge should also be able to tell a fresh result from the last good value.

**Exact change — web/api:** give the shared proof visual a state contract: `loading`, `ready`, `empty`, `stale`, `error`. Loading: fixed skeleton plus **“Reading Tempo…”**. Empty after a successful read: **“No transfers in this window.”** Stale with cached data: keep the value, a quiet amber dot and **“Last checked 2m ago.”** Failed without data: **“Couldn’t read Tempo”** plus a small Retry action. Scope bounded activity totals as **“Recent swaps observed”** unless the entire stated period was read. For the US→MX illustration, say **“Illustrative route · on-chain OUSD→xMXN swaps”**; token movements do not prove the people’s geography. Do not pulse Retry failures or turn unknown values into zero.

Evidence: archived `home/built-on-tempo.tsx:113–139,147–180,206–218`. This is source-backed state coverage, not a claim that an outage occurred during the normal matrix. Preserve Invest’s existing explicit history error/retry behavior.

**12. Micro-interactions: make proof inspection and receipt arrival feel finished.**

The kit already has a copy check/toast, sliding tabs, row feedback, Card flip/shine and new-activity animation. Recommending more of those indiscriminately would add noise. The next improvement is consistency: what is clickable, where feedback appears, and how a confirmed amount becomes a receipt.

**Exact change — web/mobile/mobile2/api:** make hover and keyboard focus expose the same accent border/background. Lift actionable tiles by at most 2px; static statistics should not lift. Give CopyAddress a 44px touch target on phones, a stable-width local check for 1.4s, and a selectable full-address fallback if clipboard access fails—the shortened address cannot satisfy “Select the address instead.” Keep a separate explorer action. When a real confirmation is observed, keep the amount visually anchored, reveal its receipt in 160ms, then insert the new activity row once by transaction/log identity. Do not replay the success effect merely because the user revisits Activity. Keep authorization feedback visually distinct from on-chain confirmation. Show measured submit-to-observed-receipt time only if recorded; otherwise say **“Confirmed on Tempo.”** No confetti or recurring shine.
