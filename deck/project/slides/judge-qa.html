Appendix for Q&A. Open with these four answers. 01 Buyer: our proposed first adopter is a local fintech or corridor operator; this deck demonstrates the product, it does not establish customer demand. Six modules because issuance, exchange and consumer flows share one settlement layer, and a pilot must prove that integrating them costs less than assembling separate providers. 02 What travels: the contracts and app modules; locally we change the currency and rate feed, funding, risk rules and licensed partners. 03 Capital: vault collateral, exchange reserves and lender funds are separate; pilot partners must establish funding and sustainable fees, and subsidized testnet yield is not revenue. Losses fall on the account that funded the position. 04 Readiness: on-chain product flows run today; fiat payout, real income access, production assets and operating arrangements still need partners. Technical follow-ups, indexed: T1: v2 is live by default on Tempo Moderato testnet: the MultiCollateralVault and the MoveAMM exchange both issue xMXN; there is a separately funded credit pool, the Card contract, and three Invest options. Tempo gives the six products one token ledger, transfers with memos, and fee sponsorship. MPP lets an HTTP client pay for a bill or an API response and get verified settlement evidence. We show these integrations on testnet; we haven't proved lower cost, adoption or production economics. T2: xMXN is a synthetic, MXN-denominated testnet token. Institutions create collateralized debt in the vault; the exchange creates a reserve liability when it sells xMXN for OUSD; drivers borrow existing tokens. There is no bank cash-out, no SPEI and no guaranteed peg, and OUSD is valued at an assumed one dollar. T3: Invest offers sOUSD (dollars, configured at 5%), sxMXN (pesos, configured at 8%) and mIDX, an equity-index token with an owner-set price and no underlying equities. Both vaults accrue from owner-funded reserves, not investment income. The accounting ignores direct donations and does not credit unfunded past yield, so a donation can't fake the rate. mIDX earns neither rate. T4: Not necessarily. If the peso strengthens, reserve coverage falls. MoveAMM blocks any mint that would leave coverage below 100%, redeems at the lower of oracle value and reserve NAV, and limits redemptions with a leaky bucket of 25% of the reserve (a burst plus continuous refill, not a strict daily ceiling). Holders can face a haircut or a wait; outside capital can recapitalize the reserve. T5: No. Vault collateral, the exchange's OUSD reserve and lender xMXN in the credit pool are separately accounted. The AMM never touches vault collateral and only redeems up to its own liability. Repaying the credit pool returns lender funds; it doesn't burn xMXN. T6: Nothing on testnet makes them trustworthy beyond the stated assumptions. Collateral prices are owner-set feeds, and FX is admin-relayed from Coinbase, which publishes no source timestamp; a keeper rejects abnormal jumps and stale data. The owner can override rates, change risk settings and feeds, list collateral, rotate the loan signer, and lend out idle cash; those powers can make positions liquidatable. Earnings and scores are demo fixtures; signed limits stop replay and over-drawing, not fraud or default. T7: It proves verified testnet settlement for that API order. Bill and airtime fulfillment is SIMULATED. The paid partner quote records the paid intent and receipt before further work, then stores and replays the completed response. That is tested single-process recovery, not crash-proof exactly-once. Buying a signed quote buys the API response, not the swap. T8: The sponsor has target, function and spender limits, fee caps and process-local quotas; restarts reset budgets, and an unsigned request can still use an eligible sender's quota. It pays fees, not purchases. The last recorded contract suite passed 141 tests, plus source verification and a bounded independent review. They have not been audited. Before real money: audited contracts, reliable oracles and liquidity, real asset and fulfillment partners, durable recovery, and legal and compliance review.
