~8s. Say this plainly. Token issuance, vault accounting, swaps, loans and the 402 payment are real testnet transactions. The collateral is Tempo's faucet OUSD: no real value, not issuer-backed, not redeemable. Staked OUSD and tokenized T-bills are demo tokens with admin-set yield and prices: no real T-bill backing and no real yield. The FX rate is real (USD/MXN 18.37 from Coinbase) but admin-relayed on-chain, not a decentralized oracle; the Uber data is synthetic, the airtime top-up and Mercado Card's card network and number are simulated (a demo card, not Visa or Mastercard), and repayment is manual with no payroll deduction. Risks we show rather than hide: a stronger peso lowers every position's ratio; the oracle rejects prices older than 1 hour, outside 5 to 50 MXN/USD, or jumping more than 20%; the vault, desk and pool are funded separately and capped (10,000 per swap, 1,500 per driver, pool at most 80% lent); OUSD's issuer controls its transfer policy. No peg, yield or credit model is claimed. Admin trust is disclosed too, on the app's Contracts page: on testnet the owner can force-set the FX rate within its band, pause the exchange and the card, rotate the credit signer (which decides who can borrow), and list new collateral; production would need a timelock and real governance.
