Every xMXN is a peso debt backed by dollar-valued collateral, so a stronger peso is the core risk, and we show it live from the Mint app's Risk tab at the real rate, USD/MXN 18.37 admin-relayed from Coinbase. System-wide, all collateral covers all Mint debt about 2.8 times (279%); the peso would have to strengthen to about 8.90 per dollar before the system as a whole falls to the 135% liquidation line. That is an aggregate number: individual positions are liquidated earlier, one by one, at their own lines (a 160% OUSD position at about 15.5; demo T-bills below 170%, on their own demo price feed). The exchange is separate: its OUSD reserve plus a 2,500 OUSD equity buffer covers the xMXN it issued at 298%, falling to 100% only at 6.16; below that, minting stops and redemptions pay pro rata, and redemptions are capped at 25% of the reserve per rolling 24 hours. The link is a real v2 stress test: the rate force-set 30% stronger (18.37 to 12.86), a dedicated 160% position liquidated on-chain, then the real rate restored. Testnet: OUSD has no real value.
